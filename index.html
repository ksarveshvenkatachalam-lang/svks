    Case studies

    Independent pieces of product work on problems common to utilities and enterprise platforms. Organisations are fictional and figures are illustrative; the thinking is mine.

    Migration strategyEnergy retail, 600,000 accounts

    Billing replatform: treating a migration as a product launch

    The request was "migrate before the licence ends". The real problem was a supplier that could not launch time of use tariffs for EV and heat pump customers. Reframing it changed the plan: four waves by customer complexity, 31 of 46 legacy features retired on usage evidence, and readiness criteria agreed before any data moved.

    Customers rarely notice a migration until a bill looks different. Designing the first post migration bill prevents more contacts than any amount of back end testing.

    Tools

    Two working tools I use to make decisions faster and in the open. They run in your browser and keep nothing.

    Cutover readiness check

    Enter the latest dry run results to get a go or no go recommendation with the reasons behind it.

    Prioritisation with delivery risk

    RICE scoring with one addition: a risk factor for changes that touch live operations or migrations. Edit any value and the ranking updates.

    IdeaReachImpactConfidence %EffortRiskScore

    Score = Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort × risk factor (low 1.0, medium 0.8, high 0.6). Reach is customers per quarter in thousands; effort is in team weeks.

    Views

    Positions I hold on how product work should be done in large, regulated organisations.

    A migration is a product launch

    Most migrations are run as IT projects measured on data moved and dates hit. Customers judge them on something else entirely: whether their bill, their tariff and their next interaction still make sense.

    Give a migration a product owner, customer success measures and a launch plan, and it stops being a cost to survive.

    Some customers can never be a rounding error

    Utilities carry a duty of care for customers on the Priority Services Register. In a migration, those records deserve their own checks, their own owner and a zero tolerance threshold.

    Percentages hide people. I would rather delay a cutover than explain a lost vulnerability flag.

    Where AI helps discovery, and where it misleads

    Language models are excellent at clustering thousands of contact transcripts into themes in minutes. They are poor at telling you which theme matters, and they will happily summarise problems customers never had.

    I use them to find patterns, then check every pattern against raw transcripts and a few real conversations.
